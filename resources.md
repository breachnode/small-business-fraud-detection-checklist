# Public Resources

Legitimate, public sources for fraud prevention and reporting. All of these are free to read and legal to cite. This list is a starting point, not an endorsement of any single organization — cross-check important claims across at least two independent sources.

## Where to report fraud

- **FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)** — ic3.gov — Report internet crime, including fraud and identity theft. File when the loss is significant, when the fraud crosses state lines, or when you want the incident on record with law enforcement.
- **Federal Trade Commission (FTC)** — reportfraud.ftc.gov — Report fraud, scams, and identity theft.
- **Your bank's fraud department** — the phone number on your card or statement. For fraud on your bank account or card, call the same day.
- **Your payment processor's fraud team** — the number on your processor's website or monthly statement. For fraud on payment processing, call the same day.
- **The affected platform's fraud or abuse reporting channel** — for fraud involving a marketplace, payment app, ad platform, or shipping carrier.
- **Local law enforcement** — for a large loss, a local suspect, or when your bank or insurer asks for a police report number.
- **The credit bureaus** — a customer whose identity was stolen should consider a fraud alert or a credit freeze with each bureau.

## Government guidance on fraud prevention

- **CISA (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency)** — cisa.gov — Advisories and guidance on phishing, business email compromise, and small-business cybersecurity.
- **FTC Business Center** — ftc.gov/business-guidance — Guidance for businesses on data security, scams targeting small businesses, and how to report fraud.
- **U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA)** — sba.gov — Cybersecurity guidance written for small businesses, including basic protections and incident planning.
- **NIST Small Business Cybersecurity Corner** — nist.gov — Plain-language cybersecurity guidance and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, sized for organizations without a security team.

## Payment and card-network guidance

- **Your card networks' merchant guidance** — the major card networks publish merchant fraud-prevention guides and chargeback rules. Your payment processor can point you to the current versions that apply to your account.
- **Your payment processor's fraud-prevention documentation** — most processors publish guidance on their fraud tools, velocity settings, and dispute processes. Read the documentation for the tools you already pay for before buying new ones.

## Frameworks and standards

- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)** — nist.gov/cyberframework — A standard framework for organizing defensive controls. The Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover functions map directly onto the structure of this repo.
- **OWASP** — owasp.org — Web application security guidance, including the Credential Stuffing Prevention Cheat Sheet and the Authentication Cheat Sheet — directly relevant to the account-takeover rules in this repo.
- **MITRE ATT&CK** — attack.mitre.org — The standard taxonomy of adversary tactics and techniques. Useful for naming what happened in a way that banks, processors, and investigators will understand.

## Further reading (public reporting)

- **FBI IC3 Annual Internet Crime Reports** — ic3.gov — Yearly statistics on reported fraud, including business email compromise and the most common schemes affecting small businesses.
- **FTC Consumer Sentinel Network reports** — ftc.gov — Data on reported fraud by type, including trends in payment fraud and identity theft.
- **Established security news outlets** (for example, Krebs on Security, BleepingComputer, The Record) — timely coverage of fraud schemes, often with primary-source reporting. Read the article, not the headline, and check whether claims are sourced.

## A note on what is NOT listed here

This list deliberately contains no illicit marketplaces, forums, or channels, no leaked datasets, no products or vendors being recommended, and no anonymous sources without provenance. The rules in this repo are written to be implemented in whatever tools you already use — no specific product is required, and none is endorsed here. If a fraud question cannot be answered from the sources above, narrow the question before looking further afield.
